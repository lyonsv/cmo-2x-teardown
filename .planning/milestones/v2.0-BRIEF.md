# Rework the CMO 2x Report — From AI Content Production to Autonomous Marketing Operations

## Your task

You are working in the repository for this report:

https://lyonsv.github.io/cmo-2x-teardown/

**Research the underlying ideas, critically reassess the analysis, and improve the actual website.** Make the changes in the codebase. Do not just produce recommendations or a rewritten Markdown document.

Preserve the existing research depth, financial modelling, technical architecture, evidence and interactive features. The goal is to make the report substantially clearer, more commercially compelling and more accurate about what needs to be built.

The audience is a sophisticated CMO, CFO or technology leader evaluating a significant investment in AI-driven marketing transformation.

The report should be understandable in five minutes, with enough depth to withstand several hours of scrutiny.

## 1. First, understand what Intercom actually did

Before changing the report, research Intercom's initiative to double R&D productivity.

Start with:

- https://ideas.fin.ai/p/2
- https://ideas.fin.ai/p/2x-nine-months-later
- https://www.intercom.com/blog/ai-is-approving-our-pull-requests-heres-how-we-made-it-safe/

Find additional primary sources where necessary.

Understand:

- What Intercom actually measured and why.
- What changed in its engineering operating model.
- The contribution of agentic coding, shared tooling, review automation and organisational change.
- How increased autonomy removed bottlenecks across the engineering lifecycle.
- How productivity, labour costs, quality and business outcomes were measured.
- What Intercom could demonstrate versus what remained uncertain.

**Extract the transferable operating principles, not superficial analogies.**

The objective is to explore what an equivalent transformation would look like for the entire marketing organisation.

Do not imply that marketing productivity can be measured as simply as merged pull requests. Investigate the measurement problem properly.

## 2. Establish the correct central thesis

The current report is too heavily framed around AI content generation and production.

The opportunity is bigger.

**We want to increase the productive capacity of a marketing organisation by progressively giving AI agents more responsibility and autonomy to execute work towards measurable business outcomes.**

This requires changing how marketing work gets done, rather than deploying isolated AI tools.

An agent should increasingly be capable of:

1. Understanding an objective and the constraints surrounding it.
2. Planning the work required to achieve it.
3. Executing across multiple existing tools and systems.
4. Evaluating its own work against policies, quality requirements and measurable results.
5. Seeking human intervention when decisions exceed its authority or confidence.
6. Observing outcomes and determining the next appropriate action.

The distinction matters:

- **AI assistance:** a marketer uses AI to complete individual tasks faster.
- **Agentic execution:** agents complete substantial workflows, with humans reviewing their work.
- **Higher autonomy:** agents independently execute, evaluate, correct and advance work within explicit boundaries, escalating exceptions.
- **Outcome-directed autonomy:** agents can choose and execute permitted actions based on performance feedback, rather than simply completing a predetermined sequence.

Higher autonomy is not automatically better. It must be justified by reliability, economic value, risk and measured outcomes.

The report must explain what needs to be built to enable this progression, where autonomy is appropriate, and how it translates into economic value.

Do not treat "agentic marketing operating system" as a sufficient explanation. Describe the software and the work in concrete, operational terms.

## 3. Define what 2x means for a CMO

Make an explicit, defensible choice about what doubling marketing productivity means.

Consider:

- Useful marketing work completed per FTE.
- Campaigns or market launches per FTE.
- Time from business objective to live campaign.
- Qualified opportunities or pipeline per marketing FTE.
- Equivalent marketing output per dollar of fully loaded cost.
- Other suitable metrics derived from the research.

Choose a practical primary metric, alongside a small number of business outcome and quality guardrail metrics. Explain their trade-offs.

Separate three things rigorously:

**Capacity:** Can the organisation execute twice as much valuable work with the same resources?

**Efficiency:** Can it execute the same work with materially fewer human hours and lower fully loaded cost?

**Effectiveness:** Does the additional capacity actually improve business outcomes such as customer acquisition, qualified pipeline, revenue or return on marketing investment?

Doubling capacity does not establish doubled revenue.

Use the distinction throughout the report, particularly in its conclusions and financial model.

## 4. Identify the actual marketing work agents should take over

Start from a CMO's responsibilities, not a list of AI capabilities.

Analyse the recurring workflows across a substantial marketing organisation, including:

- Market and customer research.
- Planning, briefing and campaign operations.
- Content and creative production.
- Channel adaptation, localisation and transcreation.
- Brand, legal and regulatory approvals.
- Publishing, distribution and activation.
- Search and answer-engine visibility.
- Experimentation and conversion optimisation.
- Performance measurement and reporting.
- Campaign monitoring, optimisation and iteration.

These are not necessarily separate products. They may be connected stages in longer-running workflows.

For each major area, establish:

- The work being performed today.
- The business objective it supports.
- The people, agencies and tools involved.
- Sources of labour, delay, coordination and rework.
- What agents could execute independently.
- What decisions remain human.
- The potential impact on hours, cost, throughput and commercial KPIs.
- What shared infrastructure would make the autonomy possible.

Prioritise the opportunities by value, feasibility, repeatability, risk and potential for measurable autonomy.

**Do not assume that every marketing function is equally automatable or that all functions must be transformed to reach 2x.**

Identify a realistic combination of interventions that could credibly produce an organisation-wide improvement.

## 5. Make the software we need to build completely clear

Introduce an early, prominent section titled:

**What needs to be built**

A reader should understand the proposed system within 60 seconds.

Start with a plain-English explanation of what it does, who uses it and how it works.

Then describe the actual product architecture.

Consider capabilities such as:

- Objective definition and outcome tracking.
- Access to organisational knowledge, customer context and brand policies.
- Planning and orchestration of work.
- Agents with permissions to act across marketing systems.
- Execution of production, operational and analytical tasks.
- Evaluation of quality, compliance and task completion.
- Human approval, exceptions and intervention.
- Workflow state, memory, provenance and auditability.
- Measurement of performance and business results.
- Feedback-driven iteration and optimisation.

Be technically concrete.

For each major capability, explain:

1. What problem it solves.
2. What software needs to exist.
3. What inputs, data and integrations it requires.
4. What agents are permitted to do.
5. How successful execution is verified.
6. How humans intervene.
7. How it contributes to increased autonomy and better outcomes.

Distinguish clearly between what we should **build and own**, what we should integrate, and what existing marketing platforms already provide.

Avoid proposing a large generic platform that must be completed before any customer gets value.

Show the smallest useful end-to-end implementation and how the architecture expands from there.

## 6. Use the content supply chain as the first concrete example

The content supply chain is a strong candidate for the initial implementation because it spans multiple teams, tools, approvals and distribution channels.

But **content generation is just one step within it**.

Model a complete content supply chain, potentially covering:

Business objective → research and planning → campaign briefing → source asset creation → adaptation and localisation → review and approval → publishing and activation → performance measurement → optimisation and refresh.

Show how agents could take responsibility for more of this chain over time.

For example, instead of a person requesting ten translated assets and manually coordinating their publication, an agent could be given an objective to launch an approved campaign in additional markets.

It would plan the work, derive variants, localise them, validate brand and compliance requirements, route necessary exceptions, coordinate publication, verify deployment and assess early performance.

Explain precisely what is autonomous and what is not.

Demonstrate how removing handoffs, coordination, repeated reviews and manual operational tasks produces greater value than merely generating individual assets faster.

**Build a substantial visual of this supply chain into the report.**

It should be a polished, responsive diagram implemented directly in the website (prefer SVG or existing visualisation components, not a static screenshot).

Show:

- The full end-to-end workflow.
- The difference between human responsibilities and agent responsibilities.
- Where agents independently execute multiple stages.
- The shared platform capabilities underneath.
- Human approval and exception boundaries.
- The loop connecting measured performance to further action.

Make it clear that the first content supply chain workflow is a **wedge into a broader autonomous marketing system**, not the limit of the opportunity.

The visual should help explain the system at a glance, rather than simply decorate the report.

## 7. Quantify the impact in actual labour and financial terms

This is a critical weakness to address.

The CMO and CFO need to know:

**How many human working hours can be removed from existing workflows, how much additional work can the organisation absorb, and what are the genuine financial implications?**

Retain and improve the report's existing financial and capacity models.

| External agency/vendor cost | | |
| AI and software cost | | |

| Cycle time | | |

Make the assumptions editable or clearly documented.

For every major figure, identify whether it is:

- Observed evidence.
- A sourced industry benchmark.
- An illustrative modelling assumption.
- A hypothesis requiring customer validation.

Do not manufacture precision or claim benchmark values without evidence.

Distinguish rigorously between:

- Hours eliminated from existing workloads.
- Capacity redeployed into additional work.
- Avoided future hires.
- Reducible external expenditure.
- Genuine removable headcount.
- Capacity already unused under baseline assumptions.

Pay particular attention to the existing review capacity model. Reconcile its reported spare capacity with any increases in total human review hours as throughput grows.

Show three economically distinct scenarios:

**A. Same team, greater output:** How much more useful work can existing marketing resources complete?

**B. Same output, lower costs:** How much human work and fully loaded expenditure can genuinely be eliminated?

**C. Growing demand, limited hiring:** How far can work volume increase without proportional increases in staffing and agency spend?

Retain the existing illustrative company and financial baseline where useful, but validate the underlying assumptions and avoid double-counting labour and agency savings.

Show the annual financial implications, required investment and sensitivity to adoption, error rates, inference costs and review overhead.

## 8. Tie autonomy to real CMO outcomes

The report must explain the chain of causality between what gets built and what the CMO values.

For example:

Higher agent autonomy → fewer manual coordination and execution steps → shorter launch cycle → more campaigns or experiments per FTE → potentially more qualified demand and faster learning.

Be explicit about where the evidence stops and the hypothesis begins.

Develop a clear scorecard covering:

**Business outcomes**
- Marketing-sourced pipeline and qualified opportunities.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Cost per qualified opportunity or acquisition.
- Conversion and marketing-attributed revenue.
- Marketing ROI / return on marketing investment.

**Operating economics**
- Useful output per marketing FTE.
- Human hours per completed workflow.
- FTE-equivalent capacity released.
- Fully loaded cost per completed workflow.
- Agency spend avoided.
- Workflow cycle time.

**Autonomy and quality**
- Percentage of eligible workflows completed autonomously.
- Human interventions per workflow.
- Review and exception handling time.
- First-pass acceptance.
- Error and compliance escape rates.
- Percentage of agent actions achieving verified objectives.

Prefer completed workflows and verified outcomes over misleading metrics such as token volume, number of assets generated or percentage of individual steps involving AI.

The scorecard should allow leadership to distinguish genuine productivity improvements from increased activity without increased value.

## 9. Explain how the system expands beyond content

This should be a meaningful part of the strategic argument.

Show how the same infrastructure built for the content supply chain can support adjacent workflows such as:

- Managing ongoing campaign execution.
- Identifying underperforming campaigns and proposing or implementing permitted changes.
- Running localisation and market-expansion programmes.
- Finding opportunities for conversion improvement.
- Conducting repeatable experimentation.
- Monitoring brand discoverability across search and AI answer engines.
- Generating and acting on performance insights.

The important idea is that reusable agent infrastructure, governance, permissions, evaluation and outcome measurement make progressively more autonomous work possible.

Be clear about which capabilities are genuinely reusable versus those requiring distinct domain-specific implementations.

**The first product must be narrow enough to build and prove. The overall programme must be broad enough to make 2x marketing productivity plausible.**

## 10. Propose a credible delivery sequence

Produce a specific roadmap:

### First 12 weeks — prove one outcome

Build and deploy an agent-enabled end-to-end workflow in a real marketing environment.

Identify:

- The workflow to target and why.
- The specific software needed.
- The integrations required.
- Baseline labour, throughput, cost and quality.
- What level of autonomy is feasible.
- The target improvement and acceptance criteria.
- How success will be demonstrated.

### Next two quarters — increase autonomy and expand

Remove the next workflow bottlenecks, reduce human interventions and extend the shared system into adjacent operations.

Show what gets built and what measurable improvements should result.

### Twelve months — organisation-wide 2x ambition

Describe the portfolio of workflow transformations, operating-model changes and supporting capabilities that might credibly achieve the productivity goal.

Quantify how the contributions combine without double-counting hours, overlapping workflows or gains that depend on the same baseline resources.

Address organisational enablement, training, trust, permissions, accountability and role redesign.

Clearly distinguish a proven pilot result from a projected organisation-wide outcome.

## 11. Make the report substantially easier to understand

Restructure the report around the questions an executive would ask:

1. **The opportunity:** What does 2x marketing productivity mean and why is it achievable?
2. **The strategic insight:** Why is increased autonomy the lever, rather than more AI-generated content?
3. **The recommendation:** What exactly do we need to build?
4. **The first workflow:** What does an autonomous content supply chain actually look like?
5. **The economics:** What changes in hours, FTEs, throughput and fully loaded costs?
6. **The CMO outcomes:** How does this connect to pipeline, customers, conversion and revenue?
7. **The broader opportunity:** How does the system expand across marketing?
8. **The roadmap:** What do we build first and how do we prove it?
9. **Risks and assumptions:** What could prevent the target being achieved?
10. **Detailed appendices:** Technical architecture, models, research, sources and supporting analysis.

Treat this as a suggested hierarchy, not an inflexible table of contents. Improve it if the argument demands something different.

The first screen of the website should communicate the thesis, the system being proposed and the measurable ambition.

Preserve the depth by using effective visual hierarchy, expandable technical detail, diagrams, tables and appendices.

Do not achieve clarity by stripping out the substance.

Avoid:

- Generic AI-transformation language.
- Overcomplicated or invented terminology.
- Claims that agents can reliably deliver revenue without evidence.
- Equating asset production with marketing productivity.
- Dense architectures without understandable workflows.
- Unexplained financial estimates.
- Lists of technologies that do not answer what is being built.

Write as a practical engineering and business leader who understands the economics of a large marketing organisation.

## Final test

The report succeeds if a CMO can read the opening sections and explain:

- **What we're building:** a concrete system for safely delegating substantial marketing workflows to agents.
- **Why it matters:** more useful work completed with less human execution and coordination.
- **Where we start:** one measurable end-to-end content supply chain workflow.
- **How it expands:** shared infrastructure enabling greater autonomy across other marketing operations.
- **What it is worth:** explicit, auditable improvements in human hours, FTE-equivalent capacity, operational costs and business KPIs.
- **How we prove it:** a credible implementation plan with measured outcomes and quality guardrails.

Above all, make the relationship between **software built → autonomy enabled → human work reduced → marketing capacity increased → business outcomes improved** unmistakably clear.

That is the report's central argument. Everything else should support it.
