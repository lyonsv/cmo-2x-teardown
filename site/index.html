Claim & measurement

The headline metric counts approved-and-live asset-variants per marketing head per quarter. The denominator is all marketing FTE: brand, operations, regional and agency-equivalent staff. Assets that are generated but not approved never count. The number is always shown beside unique concepts and variants per concept, so growth from new ideas cannot hide behind growth from cheap copies.

The count is of approved-and-live variants only. To reach it the model derives more variants than it publishes, at an approval yield of 95% Modelled; the next section costs the rejected ones.

What 2x does not mean

It does not mean twice as many assets generated, twice the budget, or half the team. It means twice as many approved-and-live asset-variants per marketing head per quarter, at flat budget, with outcomes that hold.

Falsifier

If approved-live variants per head double but holdout-measured conversion/CAC does not improve and first-pass compliance falls within 2–3 quarters, the 2x claim is false for that org.

02

The CFO answer

Flat budget

Gartner reports marketing budgets at 7.7% Benchmark of company revenue in its earlier survey and 7.8% Benchmark in its latest, from a self-reported survey of 402 Benchmark CMOs in North America, the UK and Europe, mostly at large companies. That is an average across respondents, not a rule for any one organisation. The consequence is the same either way: budgets are not rising to meet the ambition, so 2x has to come from what each approved asset costs, not from new money.

Cost per approved, live asset

Fully loaded, an approved, live asset falls from $64.71 Modelled to $41.18 Modelled, a change of −36% Modelled. The cost is built from six components: generation, review, rework, tooling, rights and storage. It includes the cost of variants that were derived and reviewed but rejected at the gate, at the approval yield of 95% Modelled. Rejected variants are costed but never counted as output.

The scope is production-attributable cost only: $2.59M Modelled a year today and $3.29M Modelled at 2x, or 3.3% Modelled and 4.2% Modelled of the marketing budget. It excludes media and non-production headcount. The saving is therefore a share of a small line, and the bigger prize is what the extra approved output does for outcomes, which the outcome case section takes up. Review is the binding constraint

The model holds at flat review headcount only if at least 41% Modelled of variants clear with no human touch. The page assumes 20% today, 60% at 2x Modelled. If the real rate falls short of the break-even share, review capacity is exceeded and the 2x programme stalls in the queue, whatever the cost per asset says.

AI spend

AI spend rises from $38,421 Modelled to $340,421 Modelled a year, a multiple of 8.9x Modelled. That is the price of the substitution, not a target to hit.

AI spend framing

AI spend is a floor, not a target. Teams that are rewarded for spending on AI will spend on AI (Goodhart's law); the measure that matters is the cost of an approved, live asset.
